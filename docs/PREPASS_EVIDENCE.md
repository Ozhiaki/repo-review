# Evidence for Prepared Review

Prepared Review promotes the evaluated candidate-2 method. It is an additional review method. Its source accuracy, usefulness, and cost must be assessed separately.

## What the comparisons establish

The saved evidence includes six stages for each method on two repositories: cli-printing-press and last30days. Both conditions used the same analytical prompts, fixed source for each pair, Sonnet 5.5, and Extra effort. The baseline condition used supplied discovery and reading tools. It was not a test of every possible Direct Review host or tool set.

Candidate-2 added a compact packet, indexed lookup, and question-based source reads. The experiment changed these together. It does not isolate their separate effects.

At the fixed experiment rates:

| Repository | Baseline estimate | Candidate estimate | Candidate change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| cli-printing-press | $9.1556067 | $8.1214459 | 11.3% lower |
| last30days | $8.3001443 | $8.4865225 | 2.25% higher |

These are model cost estimates, not invoices or total operating costs. L3 includes a usage stop and resumed context. That interruption affects the cost comparison. Preparation costs remain separate. The results do not establish a general saving.

The candidate requested fewer distinct source paths and made more tool calls in both pairs. Many extra calls were targeted lookups. This does not by itself establish greater depth or better quality.

## Assess useful differences

The candidate often gave more attention to checks, transitions, and failure controls in these runs. The baseline often gave more attention to product promises and integration components. Both showed useful examples of the other tendency.

Agent choice, model, reasoning setting, and prior context can also change the Direct Review result. These two pairs do not establish fixed method personalities or a reliable rule for repository classes.

Different extraction lists are useful when the ideas have source support, a clear independent purpose, and credible boundaries. Reproducing the baseline list is not a quality requirement. The old experiment scoring protocol remains historical evidence.

Both methods made source errors. The candidate's L3 lift said no test varies engagement. A test at line 63 did. Its relevant read covered another range. The baseline's L3 lift attributed effects on later batches to a semaphore that is local to each invocation. These errors show why source claims need checks independent of the value of the selected extraction ideas.
